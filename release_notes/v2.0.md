## Why v2.0

v2.0 is a **scientific-correction release**, not a polish pass.
Paper IV of the series -- *Optical-Axis Anisotropy on the A=1
Discrete Causal Lattice: Birefringence Cancellation and a
Single-Domain No-Go* -- establishes a no-go result that materially
corrects the substrate this paper's derivation is carried out on.

The finding, in one sentence: the original single-domain
three-diagonal hop set yields an **order-unity common-mode
photon-speed anisotropy** -- an optical axis that is **observable
but is not observed** -- and is therefore excluded as a physical
vacuum construction.

**The A=1 conservation principle is not falsified.** The failure
traces to the hop-set *realization* -- the omission of the fourth
cube body diagonal `V_4 = (1,1,-1)` -- not to the conservation law.
The lattice geometry must change; the successor architecture is not
birefringent.

The bump to 2.0 (rather than 1.1) reflects that the derivation's
substrate is superseded, not merely annotated.

## What did NOT change

The paper body, appendices, figures, notes, and all verification
scripts are unchanged. They document the reasoning that led to the
falsifying result and are deliberately not rewritten. Where the
front-matter revision notice qualifies a body claim, **the notice
is authoritative**.

The algebraic results are unaffected by the no-go: containment
`⊇`, the dimension-71 centralizer
`su(6) ⊕ su(6) ⊕ u(1)`, and the factor-product projection are
algebraic and do not depend on the hop set's phenomenological
viability.

Results that *do* depend on the specific gauge-sector realization
are to be read conditionally per the notice -- the birefringence
corollary among them, since it **is** the excluded anisotropy. The
`g_3^2 / g_2^2 = 3/2` ratio is deliberately **left open** rather
than settled either way: the Q-tensor and the universal prefactor
cancel in the ratio (which is why the paper calls it independent of
the one-loop prefactor `c`), so it may survive the no-go -- but the
successor geometry could change the spectator counting it rests on.
No row in the changes table asserts either outcome.

## Build-system changes (non-scientific)

`make` wrote to `build/` and `stage/` while the repo's actual
directories were `.build/` and `.stage/`, so the make targets and
the on-disk layout had drifted apart.

- `common.mak`: `build_dir` -> `.build`, `stage_dir` -> `.stage`.
- `makefile`: `DOC_TITLE` set to the repo name; new `VERSION`
  variable; `promote` now emits the version-stamped
  `.stage/<DOC_TITLE>-v<VERSION>.pdf`.
- **`make clean` no longer deletes `.stage/`.** It previously
  removed both directories; once `stage_dir` pointed at the durable
  archive that would have destroyed every released PDF.
- `.gitignore`: `.build/` was never ignored; now it is.
- Docs updated to match (`README.md`, `data/README.md`,
  `release_notes/README.md`, `CLAUDE.md`).
  `release_notes/v1.0.md` is left alone as a historical record.
