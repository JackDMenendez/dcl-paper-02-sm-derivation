\paragraph{Programme.}  The A=1 Discrete Causal Lattice series
begins from a single conservation axiom on a discrete substrate
(the unity constraint $\mathcal{A}=1$ on the bipartite octahedral
lattice $\mathcal{T}_\diamond^3$) and works out what physics
follows.  Paper~I (\emph{Geometry First}~\cite{menendez2026geometry},
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20078529}%
     {DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.20078529})
established the substrate and derived the Dirac equation, the Born
rule, hydrogen quantization at four significant figures, and the
structural form of the induced gauge action --- without post-hoc
parameter fitting; the audit table of Paper~I records each
derivation against its supporting experiment.  Reading the present
paper without Paper~I in hand is possible but unrewarding.

Paper~II takes up the central open conjecture of Paper~I
(§15, Eq.~(137)):
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:automorphism_conjecture}
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{T}_\diamond^3, \mathcal{A}=1)
\;=\; SO(3,1) \times SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1).
\end{equation}
%
Both sides are precise finite-dimensional Lie algebras with real
dimension $18 = 6 + 8 + 3 + 1$.  The match is more than dimensional:
the structure constants of the lattice's automorphism algebra must
reproduce those of the right-hand side factor by factor.  Either the
automorphism calculation closes the equality (and the Standard Model
gauge group is derived from a single conservation axiom on a discrete
substrate), or it does not (and Paper~II is a precise statement of
the obstruction).  Both outcomes are concrete results.

\subsection{Continuing Paper~I's audit-table convention}
\label{subsec:audit_convention}

Paper~II continues the audit-table convention established in
Paper~I: every claim of the paper appears as a row in
Table~\ref{tab:audit} with a status tag (\texttt{PASS} =
symbolically verified with no open qualifiers; \texttt{PART} =
mechanism demonstrated, full quantitative match still pending;
\texttt{STUB} = stated result with no verification yet;
\texttt{FAIL} = tested and disconfirmed under the stated ansatz)
and a pointer to the evidence backing it.  Paper~I's table mapped predicted
physical phenomena to numerical experiments and tracked
quantitative match against measurement; Paper~II adapts the
convention to a derivation paper, mapping structural sub-claims of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:automorphism_conjecture} to symbolic-verification
scripts in \nolinkurl{src/utilities/} and tracking whether the
relevant Lie-algebra statement is symbolically verified, partially
established, conjectural, or disconfirmed.  In both papers, prose defers to the table; the
table is the canonical record.

Three operational consequences of the convention bear directly on
how the present paper is read:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Reproducibility.}  Each \texttt{PASS} row carries a
sympy-verified statement reproducible from the repository.  A
reader who doubts a claim can run the corresponding script and
either confirm the row or report a bug.  Prose fluency is
decoupled from claim verification.

\item \emph{Negative results stay visible.}  A \texttt{FAIL} row
is part of the paper's contribution, not an embarrassment to be
elided.  The structural impossibility of SM-style $CP$ as a
discrete symmetry of the lattice tick rule
(\nolinkurl{tick_rule_cp_modified.py}, a \texttt{FAIL} row in
Table~\ref{tab:audit}) is precisely this kind of result.

\item \emph{Drift prevention.}  A prose claim that overstates a
sub-claim's status is mechanically detectable by comparing the
prose to the table's status tag.  Paper~II's prose was reviewed
against Table~\ref{tab:audit} on this standard before each commit.
\end{itemize}

Readers may wish to navigate Table~\ref{tab:audit} first and use
it as a structural index to the rest of the paper.

\subsection{Open subproblems}
\label{subsec:open_subproblems}

What Paper~II takes up:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Phase~3: Exact equality vs containment} in
$\mathrm{Aut}_\text{ext}$.  The verified statement is
$\supseteq$; the conjecture asserts $=$.  Whether the extended
structure admits additional symmetries beyond the four-factor
product is a finite Lie-algebra enumeration question constrained by
commutation with the established factors.

\item \textbf{Phase~1 (with sub-task~0 and Phase~1.5): SM-chirality
coupling alignment} --- partially resolved during the present work.  The Standard Model's $SU(2)_W$
couples only to left-handed fermion doublets; right-handed fermions
are $SU(2)_W$ singlets.  Whether the framework's bipartite RGB/CMY
parity is the SM's chirality projector, and whether the proposed
$SU(2)_W$ couples asymmetrically to $\psi_R$ versus $\psi_L$ as
required by the SM, was the load-bearing structural-alignment
problem entering Paper~II.  Phase~1 of the present work establishes
that the bipartite parity is precisely spatial parity (not chirality
projection) and that the proposed $SU(2)_W$ couples vector-like
(not chirally); Phase~1.5 rules out the natural antilinear
tick-rule modification that might have recovered SM-style $CP$.
The remaining alignment question --- whether SM chirality can be
recovered by some further structural enrichment of the lattice ---
is open but no longer load-bearing for Eq.~\eqref{eq:automorphism_conjecture}
itself, whose Lie-algebra statement is consistent with the
characterisation obtained.  See Section~\ref{sec:established_factors}
and Table~\ref{tab:audit}.

\item \textbf{Phase~4: Explicit $1/g^2$ prefactor} for the
$SU(2)_W$ and $SU(3)$ Wilson actions on the bipartite octahedral
lattice.  The existing $U(1)$ induced-gauge-action calculation
(Paper~I Appendix~B) supplies the template; the non-abelian
generalisation goes through with matrix-valued link variables.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope and outcomes}
\label{subsec:scope}

Paper~II's scope is precisely the three subproblems above.  All
other open questions noted in Paper~I --- the Farey-sequence mass
spectrum (Paper~I follow-on \#3), the Veneziano connection
(\#3), the proton-interior structure (\#16 dependency), plasma /
gradient ionization (\#1), and the like --- are out of scope and
deferred to subsequent papers in the series.

The paper has two viable terminal states.  If the three
subproblems resolve in the same direction, the conjecture closes and
the Standard Model gauge group is derived; the paper publishes that
derivation.  If any subproblem fails, the paper publishes a precise
statement of the obstruction and the framework's verified scope
(\emph{relativistic Hilbert-space + gravity + Born-rule structures}
plus $SO(3,1) \times U(1)$ on the existing per-site $\mathbb{C}^2$,
per Paper~I) is restated unchanged.
